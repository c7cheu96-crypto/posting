Spicy Webfoot Octopus & Charcoal-Grilled Hagfish at Jjudwaejip in Dongtan

If you love spicy food, smoky grills and the fun of building every bite with your own hands, let me take you to a little spot I visited in Dongtan: Jjudwaejip (쭈돼집).

Dongtan is a newer city in Hwaseong, Gyeonggi Province, just south of Seoul. The name Jjudwaejip is a playful mash-up: jju from jjukkumi (webfoot octopus), dwae from dwaeji (pork), and jip meaning "house". So, the octopus-and-pork house!

The Table Setting

Like most Korean restaurants, the meal starts with free side dishes. Ours came in a neat divided tray: a sheet of seaweed, thin slices of sweet-and-sour pickled radish (musam, 무쌈) for wrapping, and a few cloves of raw garlic, with little dishes of sauce on the side.

Wrap It Up: Ssam Time

Then came a generous bowl of seasoned seaweed flakes and a bundle of perilla leaves (kkaennip, 깻잎), a fragrant, slightly minty leaf Koreans love. This is all for ssam (쌈), the Korean habit of wrapping food in a leaf with a little garlic or pickle and eating it in one bite.

My number one tip here: wrap everything. The food really does taste better as ssam.

Stir-Fried Jjukkumi (쭈꾸미 볶음)

Jjukkumi is a small octopus, smaller and more tender than regular octopus, and a favorite in spicy Korean stir-fries. Ours arrived on a hot plate with a big pile of bean sprouts, and true to the restaurant's name, there was pork in there too.

The red chili sauce soaks into the sprouts, and you get chewy octopus and rich pork in the same mouthful. Put some in a perilla leaf with a pinch of seaweed flakes and you have the perfect bite.

Charcoal-Grilled Hagfish (꼼장어)

This was the star for me. Kkomjangeo is hagfish, often sold as "slime eel" in English. Don't let that put you off! In Korea it's a classic drinking snack, usually marinated in a spicy sauce and grilled at the table.

Here it cooks over real charcoal, and you can taste it. The smoky charcoal flavor really comes through. The edges of the sauce caramelize a little, the texture is pleasantly chewy, and it goes very well with a cold beer or soju.

Good to Know

  • Eat it as ssam: wrap the dishes in perilla leaves with radish or garlic.
  • Seaweed flakes and perilla leaves come with the meal, so there's plenty to wrap with.
  • The hagfish is grilled over charcoal, so expect a little smoke and a lot of flavor.

Verdict

Jjudwaejip is a cozy, no-fuss place for spicy octopus and smoky charcoal-grilled hagfish, with plenty of wraps to go around. If you find yourself in Dongtan and want a real Korean dinner, it's well worth a visit. Grab some perilla leaves and dig in!
